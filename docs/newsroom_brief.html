The job your desk already does every week

A new poll drops, or an alliance shifts. A political editor has to decide, on deadline, whether it actually matters, which assumptions just changed, and what the desk can safely say on air or in print. Psephos does that read for you each week — with every claim linked to a source you can check before you publish.

What your desk gets

A weekly briefing & alertsA short, plain-English read on the race, plus a same-day alert when the evidence materially changes — so you're never caught flat-footed by a poll or a defection.
A publishable chartA clean seat-range graphic your graphics team can drop straight into coverage, refreshed as the forecast moves.
A forecast page you can verifyIts own shareable link, with the latest number, every past version, the exact resolution rule, and the data and proof open for a signed-out reader.
Wording you can stand behindApproved, defensible language for each forecast — including "too close to call" when that's the honest answer, which keeps your desk out of a bad headline.

Why you can trust the number

  • It tells you when not to call it. When independent methods genuinely disagree, the verdict is "too close to call," and it shows the split. No manufactured number to fill a lower-third.
  • It's verifiable, not "trust me." Every forecast is Bitcoin-timestamped before the event, and the underlying data downloads from the page. Your audience can check it themselves, without an account.
  • It keeps its own scorecard — misses included. Calls and "too close to calls" alike are graded after the result, and wrong calls stay public forever. That honesty is the whole point of using it.

How it handles the hard parts

Uncertainty is a first-class answer, not a gap. Corrections reach you the same working day, by the same channel, and nothing is quietly overwritten. Election-law windows (ECI silence and polling periods) are agreed with you up front — horserace output is withheld when the rules require it, because your newsroom carries the publishing liability, not us.
