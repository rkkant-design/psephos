Why now — a legally cleared field

India had a massive, proven appetite for event prediction: opinion-trading apps (Probo, TradeX, SportsBaazi) grew to tens of millions of users letting people stake money on yes/no questions. In 2025–26 the law removed all of it: the Online Gaming Act classified them as money games, the apps shut their real-money business, and the government blocked Polymarket and Kalshi for Indian users. The demand did not disappear — its supply did.

There is no FiveThirtyEight of India, no Mantic of India. What exists: TV pollsters (content, not product), political consultancies (services, not instruments), and astrology apps. Globally, Mantic raised $29M on AI forecasting with zero India presence and a single-method engine; Psephos already runs four independent methods with a measured reason they must be combined.

What we have already built

The engineFour instruments per question: a structural model (reads 60 years of results, no news), bias-corrected polls, de-vigged markets, and a protocol-driven research pass. 10,000-run simulations; a CALL only when the methods independently clear 95%.
The record46 elections back-tested (zero wrong gate calls), a real out-of-sample cycle in 2026 (the one miss-pattern became a safety rule now active in Punjab), Brier-scored probabilities including the open ones, and a lifetime 34/37 on certified calls.
The trust machineryEvery forecast SHA-256-hashed at freeze, anchored into Bitcoin via OpenTimestamps, misses displayed in red forever. "We called it in October" becomes checkable by strangers — no competitor in this market can say that.
The productA live app: searchable forecasts, a moments calendar (elections, matches, launches), free-form Ask with instant protocol-driven estimates, weekly self-updating autopilot, and a public changelog. Entered independent benchmarks (ForecastBench) against the world's best AI forecasters.

Go-to-market — a credibility ladder with dated rungs

Why this is defensible

A provable recordSelf-reported accuracy is marketing; a Bitcoin-anchored ledger is evidence. A competitor can copy features, not two years of sealed history.
Abstention as the productWe certify which forecasts can be acted on and refuse the rest. Anyone whose revenue depends on always answering cannot copy a product built on refusing.
Structural depthConstituency-level models, measured alliance-transfer coefficients, pollster-error ledgers — numbers that live in decades of ingested results, not in news a generalist AI can read.
India home fieldThe data, the languages, the election rhythm, the displaced audience — and a measured science (our instruments' errors correlate at 0.745; we publish it) that global single-method rivals do not.

Risks, stated plainly

The record is young. 37 resolved certified calls is proof of method, not yet statistical proof of 95% precision; that takes ~60+, which the weekly match forecasts and the 2027 cycle will supply. Regulation: publishing forecasts near polling dates is restricted (silence periods); compliance is designed into the media product, and we sell information, never wagering. Cyclicality: elections cluster; sport and world events exist in the product to smooth engagement and scoring between cycles. Instruments inherit India's poll quality; that is exactly why the engine corrects pollsters with their measured errors and refuses to certify through bad data.

What we need to go from engine to business

  1. A green light to productionize: move the app from a private page to our own domain on the standard stack, with the engine running server-side (est. a few weeks of focused build).
  2. A small operating budget: data sources, compute, and a legal review of the media product's silence-period workflow.
  3. Two media conversations before January: the Feb-Mar 2027 scorecard is the pitch; a pilot signed before the cycle rides the proof as it happens.
  4. A decision on identity: we currently compete in global benchmarks anonymously; going public with the brand is a step we should take deliberately, ideally on the back of the midterms and first benchmark scores.
