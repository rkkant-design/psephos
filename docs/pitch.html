Why now — a legally cleared field

India had a massive, proven appetite for event prediction: opinion-trading apps (Probo, TradeX, SportsBaazi) grew to tens of millions of users letting people stake money on yes/no questions. In 2025–26 the law removed all of it: the Online Gaming Act classified them as money games, the apps shut their real-money business, and the government blocked Polymarket and Kalshi for Indian users. The demand did not disappear — its supply did.

There is no FiveThirtyEight of India, no Mantic of India. What exists: TV pollsters (content, not product), political consultancies (services, not instruments), and astrology apps. Globally, Mantic raised $29M on AI forecasting with zero India presence and a single-method engine; Psephos already runs four independent methods with a measured reason they must be combined.

What I've built, solo

The engineFour instruments per question: a structural model (reads 60 years of results, no news), bias-corrected polls, de-vigged markets, and a protocol-driven research pass. 10,000-run simulations; a CALL only when the methods independently clear 95%.
The record46 elections back-tested (zero wrong gate calls), a real out-of-sample cycle in 2026 (the one miss-pattern became a safety rule now active in Punjab), Brier-scored probabilities including the open ones, and a lifetime 60/63 on certified calls.
The trust machineryEvery forecast SHA-256-hashed at freeze and sealed into an independent public timestamp (the Bitcoin blockchain via OpenTimestamps — used as a public notary, not currency; only a fingerprint is stored). Misses in red forever. "Called it in October" becomes checkable by strangers — no competitor here can say that.
The productA live app: searchable forecasts, a moments calendar (elections, matches, launches), free-form Ask with instant protocol-driven estimates, weekly self-updating autopilot, and a public changelog. Entered independent benchmarks (ForecastBench) against the world's best AI forecasters.

Where I'd start — the newsroom wedge

The first customer is a newsroom covering an election. When a poll drops or an alliance flips, a political desk has to decide whether it matters and what it can safely publish. Psephos gives them a weekly briefing, a publishable chart, and a forecast page they can verify — plus the discipline to say "too close to call" when it is. No Indian pollster offers a checkable record; that is the opening.

Go-to-market — a credibility ladder with dated rungs

Why this is defensible

A provable recordSelf-reported accuracy is marketing; an independently timestamped, tamper-proof ledger is evidence. A rival can copy the method — it isn't secret — but not the dated, scored history that makes a single number trustworthy.
Abstention as the productI certify which forecasts can be acted on and refuse the rest. Anyone whose revenue depends on always giving a confident number cannot copy a product built on refusing.
Structural depthConstituency-level models, measured alliance-transfer coefficients, pollster-error ledgers — numbers that live in decades of ingested results, not in news a generalist AI can read.
India home fieldThe data, the languages, the election rhythm — and a measured science (the instruments' errors correlate at 0.745; I publish it) that global single-method rivals do not.

Risks, stated plainly

The record is young. 63 resolved certified calls is strong proof of method, not yet statistical proof of 95% precision; that takes more, which the weekly match forecasts and the 2027 cycle will supply. Regulation: publishing forecasts near polling dates is restricted (silence periods); compliance is designed into the newsroom product, and I sell information, never wagering. Solo founder: this is built and run by one person today — the pilots will show me where help is needed first. Instruments inherit India's poll quality; that is exactly why the engine corrects pollsters with their measured errors and refuses to certify through bad data.
