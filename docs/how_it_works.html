How a forecast earns the right to be trusted

Most forecasters give you a confident answer. Psephos gives you a calibrated one — and, when the evidence genuinely isn't there, it refuses to answer at all. This page shows the machinery, step by step.

01Every question is answered several independent ways

The whole design rests on one measured fact: methods that read the same news fail together. So Psephos never trusts one method. Each question — “Who wins Uttar Pradesh?”, “Who controls the US House?” — is answered by instruments that cannot fail the same way, because they read different worlds:

02For elections: 10,000 parliaments, not one guess

An Indian election isn't one prediction — it's 403 constituency races where a 3-point vote swing can move 100 seats. So the engine simulates the entire election ten thousand times, each run jittered by realistic, measured error, and counts what happens:

03The gate: a CALL must be earned by agreement

Here is the heart of it. Across 154 resolved questions we measured that our instruments err on the same side 92% of the time — when the information environment is wrong, everything downstream of it is wrong together. Which means one instrument saying “95% sure” is not actually 95%. The gate prices that in:

This rule has already paid for itself. In Tamil Nadu 2026, a brand-new party (TVK) won 35% from nowhere — the one scenario every instrument misses together. The safety flag now suppresses calls in any such election; it is active in Punjab today, where Amritpal Singh's new party polls 8–14% with zero history. And in West Bengal 2026, every poll had the wrong winner — the gate's abstention was the only honest output in the country.
